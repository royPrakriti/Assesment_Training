THE NATIONAL MUSEUM, NEW DELHI

The National Museum in New Delhi was established in 1949. It is one of
India’s major museums and contains collections representing many periods
of the country’s history. The museum is located on Janpath and is
administered by the Ministry of Culture, Government of India.

The museum’s galleries cover archaeology, manuscripts, coins, arms and
armour, decorative arts, paintings, and textiles. Together, these
collections provide an overview of India’s artistic traditions,
religious practices, technology, and everyday life.

The Harappan Gallery presents objects from the Indus Valley
Civilization. These include pottery, seals, ornaments, tools, and
figurines. Such objects help researchers study urban planning, trade,
craft production, and social life in ancient cities.

The museum also has an important collection of Buddhist art. Sculptures
and relic-related objects illustrate the spread and development of
Buddhist traditions across different regions. Stone, bronze, and
terracotta works show how artistic styles changed over time.

Its miniature painting collections include works associated with several
regional schools. These paintings often depict court life, religious
stories, landscapes, and literary themes. The manuscripts section
preserves handwritten materials that are valuable for the study of
languages, literature, and history.

Coins are another important part of the collection. They provide
evidence about rulers, trade networks, political authority, and economic
conditions. Arms and armour displays show the materials and techniques
used to make weapons and protective equipment in different historical
periods.

Conservation is essential to the museum’s work. Paper, textiles, metals,
paintings, and stone objects require different methods of care.
Environmental monitoring, careful handling, documentation, and
scientific study help protect these objects from deterioration.

The National Museum serves students, researchers, and the general
public. Through exhibitions and educational activities, it encourages
visitors to explore India’s diverse cultural heritage and understand why
preserving historical objects matters.
